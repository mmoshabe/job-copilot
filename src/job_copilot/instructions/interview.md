# Interview preparation pack

The candidate has an interview for this job. Prepare them to be the strongest candidate in the room for every interviewer they may meet: the recruiter, the hiring manager and the business owner. Use only the job post and the candidate's evidence.

## What to produce

### Company brief
What the company does, who its customers are, why this role probably exists now, and what the first 90 days likely involve. **Label every point that isn't stated in the post as an inference to verify.** Suggest 3 things to check on the company's site or LinkedIn before the interview.

### Knowledge base (what to study)
5-8 topics this role relies on (tools, domain concepts, processes named or implied by the post). For each: why it matters here, a short study note (the concepts an interviewer would expect them to know), and the candidate's closest real experience to connect it to. Prioritise topics where the evidence is thin.

### Supporting projects (talking points)
For the 3-5 requirements that matter most, the project or role from the evidence that best proves each, with a 30-second talking point in STAR form (situation, task, action, result) using **only real details and numbers**. Where the evidence is thin, add a **mini project idea**: something buildable in a weekend that would honestly demonstrate the skill, and what to show.

### Likely questions, by interviewer
- **Recruiter** (4-5): motivation, logistics, salary expectations, notice period, knockout checks.
- **Hiring manager** (6-8): technical and scenario questions on the core responsibilities, plus behavioural questions (incidents, conflict, failure, prioritisation).
- **Business owner** (3-4): value, ownership, impact, how they'd make the role pay for itself.

For each: why they ask it (what they're really checking), an answer outline built from the evidence, and the evidence it draws on. For a question that targets a gap, give an honest answer strategy.

### Questions to ask them
2-3 sharp questions for each interviewer that show understanding of the role and help the candidate judge the job.

### Red flags to probe
Anything in the post worth clarifying before accepting (scope, pay, expectations).
